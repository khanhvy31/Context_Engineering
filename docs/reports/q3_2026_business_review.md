# Q3 2026 Business Review

Prepared by: Strategy & Operations
Audience: Leadership team and department heads
Period covered: July 1 to September 30, 2026

## 1. Executive summary

Q3 2026 was the busiest quarter in Brightcart's history. Order volume grew strongly compared with the same quarter last year, helped by the summer sale, a wider product range in outdoor and kitchen, and the full launch of the Brightcart Rewards loyalty program. We also opened our second fulfilment center in Columbus, which shortened delivery times for customers on the East Coast.

The quarter also brought challenges. Rapid growth stretched our support team in August, a supplier issue affected a popular cookware line, and marketing costs rose as competition for paid social inventory increased ahead of the holiday season. This review covers each area of the business, the decisions we made, and our priorities for Q4.

## 2. Marketing

### 2.1 Channel performance

Paid social remained our largest acquisition channel, but its share of new customers fell for the second quarter in a row. Costs per click on Meta increased through the quarter, especially in September, as larger retailers began holiday campaigns early. In response, we moved budget toward creator partnerships, which continued to deliver lower acquisition costs than traditional paid social.

Search performed in line with plan. Brand search volume grew again, and we reduced spending on competitor keywords after tests showed weak returns. Pinterest was a small but efficient channel for the home category, and we plan to test it in more categories next year.

### 2.2 Summer sale

The summer sale ran for ten days in July and was our largest promotional event so far. It brought in a record number of first-time customers. Discount depth was slightly lower than last year, which helped protect margins. The team noted that customers acquired during the sale had a lower repeat purchase rate in the first 60 days than customers acquired at full price, so we will treat sale-acquired customers as a separate group in retention planning.

### 2.3 Email and lifecycle

The lifecycle team launched new browse-abandonment and replenishment emails. Replenishment emails for consumable products, such as coffee filters and cleaning supplies, performed especially well. We also cleaned up our email list, removing addresses that had not opened an email in over a year. This lowered the list size but improved deliverability.

## 3. Product and merchandising

### 3.1 Range expansion

We added roughly 400 new products this quarter, mostly in outdoor living and kitchen. The outdoor range sold through faster than expected, and several items sold out in August. Kitchen performed well overall, although the cookware supplier issue described in section 6 affected one of our top sellers.

### 3.2 Product content

We finished rewriting product pages for the top 200 products. The new pages include clearer dimensions, materials, and care instructions, along with more customer photos. Early results show fewer "is this the right size" support contacts for the updated products.

### 3.3 Brightcart Rewards

The loyalty program moved from pilot to full launch on July 15. Members earn points on every completed order and get early access to sales. Enrollment passed our internal goal within six weeks. Members order more often than non-members, although it is too early to know how much of that is caused by the program and how much reflects which customers chose to join.

## 4. Targets and scorecard

This section restates the targets the leadership team set for 2026 so that department plans for Q4 stay aligned. Actual results for each metric are tracked in the analytics warehouse and in the monthly dashboards, so they are not repeated here.

Order growth remains our headline goal, with a target of 30% year over year. We also set a goal of keeping average order value at $58 or higher as we expand into lower-priced categories. Our 2026 target for refund rate is below 3.0%. We raised this target from last year's level to reflect the larger share of orders now coming from categories such as apparel and outdoor, where returns are naturally more common. For monthly active users, the goal is 4,500 by December, and for support we aim to keep first response time under 3 hours.

Department heads should use these targets when preparing their Q4 plans. Any proposal to change a target should go through the quarterly planning process rather than being handled within a single team.

## 5. Operations and logistics

### 5.1 Columbus fulfilment center

The Columbus fulfilment center started shipping orders in late July. It now handles most orders for customers east of the Mississippi. Average delivery time for East Coast customers improved by more than a day. Opening the site went mostly to plan, although we had a slow first two weeks while staff were trained on the new warehouse management system.

### 5.2 Carriers

We renegotiated rates with our main carrier, which offset part of the increase in fuel surcharges. A regional carrier added in the Midwest performed well and will take on more volume in Q4. Damage rates in transit stayed low, helped by new packaging for glass and ceramic items.

### 5.3 Inventory

Strong demand for outdoor products led to stockouts in August. The planning team is updating its forecasting approach to react faster to early sales signals. At the same time, we ended the quarter with too much inventory in some apparel lines, which we plan to clear through targeted promotions rather than sitewide discounts.

## 6. Suppliers and quality

In August, a supplier of one of our best-selling cookware sets reported a coating defect affecting a single production batch. We paused sales of the affected set, contacted customers who had bought it, and offered replacements. The supplier has since fixed the issue, and the product returned to the site in mid-September. We are adding stricter batch testing for cookware and small appliances as a result.

We also started a review of our top 20 suppliers covering delivery reliability, quality, and sustainability practices. The review will finish in Q4.

## 7. Customer experience

### 7.1 Support volume

Support ticket volume rose sharply in August, driven by questions about delivery times, the cookware issue, and the new rewards program. First response time went above our service level for about two weeks. We brought in temporary agents and expanded our help center articles, and response times returned to normal by early September.

### 7.2 Self-service

The order tracking page and the new returns portal now handle a large share of routine requests. The returns portal lets customers print labels and choose between a refund and store credit without contacting support. Adoption has been strong, and we plan to add exchange options next quarter.

### 7.3 Customer feedback

Net promoter score dipped slightly in August and recovered in September. The most common positive comments mention product quality and fast delivery from Columbus. The most common negative comments mention slow support responses in August and out-of-stock items.

## 8. Technology

The engineering team completed the move of our product catalog to a new search service, which improved search relevance and page speed. We also launched a redesigned mobile checkout that cut the number of steps from five to three. Work on the new analytics warehouse continued, and the core order and refund tables are now updated every hour instead of every night.

## 9. People and organization

We hired 18 people this quarter, including a new VP of Operations, a second data analyst, and staff for the Columbus site. Employee turnover stayed low. We ran our first company-wide offsite since 2024 in September, which focused on our three-year plan and on improving how teams share information.

Open roles for Q4 include a senior backend engineer, a merchandising manager for outdoor, and seasonal warehouse staff for the holiday peak.

## 10. Priorities for Q4 2026

1. **Holiday readiness.** Make sure both fulfilment centers, our carriers, and the support team are ready for peak volume from mid-November to late December.
2. **Inventory balance.** Avoid outdoor stockouts while clearing excess apparel through targeted promotions.
3. **Rewards program.** Launch the first members-only event and measure its effect on repeat purchases.
4. **Marketing efficiency.** Keep shifting budget toward creators and lifecycle marketing as paid social costs rise.
5. **Exchanges.** Add exchange options to the returns portal before the holiday season.

## 11. Risks and open questions

- Paid social costs may keep rising through the holidays, putting pressure on acquisition targets.
- Peak season could again stretch the support team if temporary hiring falls behind.
- The effect of the rewards program on long-term customer value is still unclear and needs a proper analysis in Q1 2027.
- Supplier quality remains a risk for fast-growing categories where we work with newer suppliers.

## Appendix: Glossary of terms used in this review

- **Completed order:** an order that was placed and not cancelled.
- **Repeat purchase rate:** the share of customers in a period who had ordered before.
- **First response time:** the time between a customer opening a support ticket and the first reply from an agent.
