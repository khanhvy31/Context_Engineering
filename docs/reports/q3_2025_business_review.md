# Q3 2025 Business Review

Prepared by: Strategy & Operations
Audience: Leadership team

## Summary

Q3 2025 was a quarter of steady growth for Brightcart. Order volume increased quarter over quarter, driven mainly by the home and kitchen categories and a successful back-to-school campaign in August. We expanded shipping to two new European markets and completed the migration of our checkout to the new payments provider. Customer satisfaction scores held steady despite a short warehouse disruption in July.

## Commercial performance

Order volume grew in every month of the quarter, with August the strongest month on record at the time. Average order value was roughly flat. The home category continued to outperform, while apparel was softer than planned as we cleared older seasonal inventory. Promotions contributed a larger share of orders than in Q2, which the team will watch closely to avoid training customers to wait for discounts.

## Marketing

Paid social remained our largest acquisition channel. We shifted part of the search budget toward creator partnerships on TikTok, which produced lower acquisition costs than expected. The email program was rebuilt around fewer, more personalized sends, and unsubscribe rates fell as a result. Brand search volume grew, which we read as a sign that awareness campaigns earlier in the year are paying off.

## Operations and logistics

The July heatwave caused a four-day slowdown at the Reno fulfilment center. Most delayed orders shipped within a week, and we issued goodwill credits to affected customers. Carrier performance improved in September after we added a second regional carrier for the West Coast. Average delivery time ended the quarter slightly better than where it started.

## Customer experience

Support ticket volume rose in July because of the shipping delays, then returned to normal levels. First response time stayed within our service levels for most of the quarter. The new self-service order tracking page launched in September and is already deflecting a meaningful share of "where is my order" contacts.

## People

We welcomed eleven new team members, including our first dedicated data engineer and a new head of customer experience. Engagement survey results were positive overall, with workload and cross-team communication flagged as areas to improve.

## 2025 targets

The leadership team agreed the following targets for 2025 at the start of the year. They remain unchanged for the rest of the year.

| Metric | 2025 target |
|---|---|
| Order growth (year over year) | +25% |
| Average order value | $55 or higher |
| Refund rate | below 2.0% |
| Monthly active users | 3,000 by December |
| Support first response time | under 4 hours |
| Net promoter score | 45 or higher |

## Priorities for Q4 2025

1. Prepare fulfilment capacity for holiday peak, including temporary staff and a backup carrier.
2. Launch the gift guide and holiday bundles in early November.
3. Finish the loyalty program pilot and decide on a full launch for 2026.
4. Improve product page content for the top 200 products to reduce returns caused by unclear sizing and dimensions.

## Risks

- Carrier capacity during peak season may be tight, which could push delivery times out.
- Heavier reliance on promotions could put pressure on margins.
- Two senior engineering roles remain open and could slow down the loyalty program.
